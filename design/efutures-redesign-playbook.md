# EFutures Website Redesign Playbook
### From a generic outsourcing site to a premium, AI-native engineering brand

Prepared for Anil Gunathilaka — September 2026

---

## 1. Where you are today (audit findings)

I reviewed **efuturesworld.com** in full. Here's the honest picture:

**What's working**
- Real credibility exists: 25+ years in business, AWS + Microsoft partnerships, ISO certification, SLASSCOM membership, 4.3–4.4 star reviews, and named client testimonials (Sinque, MBT, Weekli, Moovparcel).
- Broad, genuinely valuable service range: AI consulting, data engineering, enterprise software, cloud, mobile, QA, cybersecurity, warehouse management.

**What's holding it back**
- **Generic outsourcing positioning.** Lines like "outsourcing that's better than insourcing" and "Hire Dedicated Developers" read like every offshore staffing vendor from 2015. It undersells 25 years of product engineering experience and buries the AI capability that should be the headline.
- **No visual identity.** The current site relies on stock layouts, placeholder-feeling stat counters (several show "0+"), and no distinctive typography, color system, or motion — nothing that would make a visitor remember the brand five minutes later.
- **Flat information architecture.** Ten services are listed side by side with no hierarchy, no case studies tied to outcomes, and no narrative arc from "problem" to "proof" to "action."
- **Weak proof-of-work.** Testimonials are quotes with no context — no logos-with-metrics, no before/after, no project depth. For an engineering company, the work itself (screens, systems, outcomes) should be the hero, not a paragraph of adjectives.
- **No content funnel.** Everything points to one generic "Contact Us," regardless of whether the visitor is a CTO evaluating vendors, a founder needing an MVP, or an enterprise buyer doing due diligence.

**The gap to close:** your three reference sites (Emilian Misera, Cuberto, Jitter) are a portfolio site, a design/dev agency, and a SaaS product — each optimized to feel crafted, confident, and current. EFutures is an enterprise engineering partner, not a boutique studio or a product company, so we won't copy them literally. We'll **borrow their design discipline** (typography-led hierarchy, restraint, motion with purpose, real work as hero content) and **apply it to enterprise credibility content** (case studies, outcomes, partnerships, AI capability) so the site reads as premium *and* trustworthy for a $250K+ enterprise software decision.

## 3. New brand direction

**Positioning shift:** from *"an outsourcing vendor with 10 services"* to *"an AI-native software engineering partner with 25 years of enterprise trust."*

**New tagline direction (pick one, or use as a starting point for your own):**
- "Software engineering, accelerated by AI."
- "25 years building software. Now built for the AI era."
- "Enterprise engineering. AI-native execution."

**Voice:** confident, specific, outcome-led. Cut words like "bespoke," "cutting-edge," "state-of-the-art," "world-class" unless followed immediately by a number or proof point. Every claim gets a receipt (a stat, a client name, a certification).

### Visual system

- **Typography-led design.** One strong, modern sans-serif (e.g., Inter, General Sans, Söhne, or Neue Montreal) at large sizes (72–120px headlines on desktop) as the dominant visual element — mirroring Emilian Misera and Jitter. Pair with a neutral body font at high readability.
- **Color palette.** A restrained base (off-white or near-black background) with one confident accent color pulled from your current brand (avoid default blue if your logo uses something distinctive) — used sparingly for links, buttons, and highlighted numbers, the way Cuberto and Jitter use a single accent against white/dark neutrals. Offer a light and dark mode if budget allows; dark mode alone for a "hero" band is a lower-cost middle ground.
- **Motion, with restraint.** Scroll-triggered fade/slide-ins for section reveals, a subtle hover state on cards and buttons, an animated number counter for stats (25+ years, XX+ projects, XX+ engineers) — this is the single highest-impact, lowest-cost motion upgrade. Avoid gimmicky cursor effects unless you have a front-end team ready to maintain them (Cuberto-style WebGL is expensive to build and maintain).
- **Imagery.** Replace generic stock photography with: (1) real product UI captured from projects you've shipped, shown as short looping clips or high-res screenshots in device frames; (2) real photos of the team/office if available — enterprise buyers trust faces and places over stock imagery.

## 4. New information architecture

Replace the current flat menu with a narrative-driven structure:

```
Home
Work          (case studies — replaces generic "Portfolio")
Services
  ├─ AI & Data Engineering
  ├─ Enterprise Software Development
  ├─ Cloud & DevOps
  ├─ Mobile Development
  ├─ QA & Cybersecurity
  └─ Dedicated Teams / Staff Augmentation
About
  ├─ Our Story
  ├─ Leadership
  └─ Careers
Insights      (blog/case studies/articles combined)
Contact
```

Group the current ten services into **five or six clear pillars** instead of listing all ten flat — this alone makes the offering feel curated rather than "we do everything."

## 6. Step-by-step execution plan

**Phase 1 — Strategy & Content (1–2 weeks)**
1. Finalize positioning, tagline, and voice (use Section 3 as a starting draft; workshop with leadership).
2. Audit and select 3–5 best client projects to feature as case studies; get client permission for logos/metrics.
3. Write final copy for Home, About, Services (5 pillar pages), and Contact using the structure in Section 5.
4. Gather real numbers for every stat currently showing "0+" (staff count, completed projects, satisfaction score).

**Phase 2 — Design (2–3 weeks)**
5. Define the design system: typography pairing, color palette, spacing scale, button/card styles, motion principles (Section 3).
6. Design the homepage first as the flagship template — hero, trust strip, services, why-us, proof-of-work, FAQ, CTA, footer.
7. Design templates for: Service detail page, Case study page, About page, Insights/blog listing + article page, Contact page.
8. Design mobile layouts for every template (mobile-first — most B2B research now starts on mobile).
9. Internal review against the reference sites: does it feel as confident and current as Cuberto/Jitter, adapted to enterprise trust rather than studio flash?

**Phase 3 — Build (3–5 weeks depending on stack)**
10. Choose a stack: a modern framework (Next.js/Astro) with a headless CMS (Sanity, Contentful, or WordPress headless) so your team can update case studies and blog posts without a developer. Add GSAP or Framer Motion for scroll animations.
11. Build the design system as reusable components first, then assemble pages.
12. Implement animations: scroll reveals, animated stat counters, smooth page transitions, hover states.
13. Optimize performance (Core Web Vitals) — motion should never come at the cost of load speed; this is a stated 2026 best practice and a direct SEO/conversion factor.
14. Implement SEO foundations: clean URLs, structured data for services/case studies, meta content mapped to buyer-journey stage (awareness/consideration/decision pages, per current B2B best practice).

**Phase 4 — Content population & QA (1–2 weeks)**
15. Populate every page with final copy and real numbers — no lorem ipsum, no "0+" left live.
16. Cross-browser and cross-device QA; test all forms and CTAs.
17. Set up analytics and conversion tracking (form starts/completions, CTA clicks, scroll depth) so you can measure the redesign's impact.

**Phase 5 — Launch & iterate (ongoing)**
18. Soft-launch to internal team and a few trusted clients for feedback.
19. Launch publicly; monitor bounce rate, time on page, and lead quality for 30 days.
20. Establish a content cadence (1–2 case studies or articles per month) to keep the "Insights" section alive — a stale blog undercuts the premium impression fast.

## 7. Practical notes

- **Don't try to out-flash Cuberto with WebGL cursor effects on day one.** That level of custom interaction design requires an ongoing front-end/creative-dev budget. Scroll-based reveal animations and one signature interaction (e.g., an animated hero graphic or a hover-triggered project preview) get you 80% of the "premium feel" at a fraction of the cost and maintenance burden.
- **Real content beats better design every time at this budget level.** If you can only do one thing well, make it the case studies — three detailed, outcome-driven case studies with real client permission will do more for credibility than any animation.
- **Keep one CTA.** Right now the site mixes "Hire Dedicated Developers," "Explore," "Book a Meeting," and a contact form. Pick one primary action ("Book a Discovery Call") and use it everywhere; offer softer secondary actions ("See Our Work," "Download Capability Deck") for visitors not ready to talk yet.
